Here is one chlorine atom.
Its outer shell has eight places for electrons.
Count them in. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven.
Seven. One place is left empty.
One electron short of a full shell.
Bring a second chlorine alongside. Same atom, same problem.
So take one. The left atom is full now. Eight.
But the right atom is two short. The hole moved. It did not close.
And the right atom pulls just as hard. Same element, same pull.
Both pull on the same electron. Nobody
wins a tug of war against themselves.
So neither of them lets go. Both hold on.
One electron from each, into the space the two shells share.
Count for the left atom. Six of its own, plus the shared pair.
Eight.
Now count for the right atom. Six of its own, plus the same pair.
Eight as well.
The same two electrons, counted twice. That is the whole trick.
That shared pair is the covalent bond.
Neither atom can afford to lose an electron, so
they share, and both of them get eight.
