Every atom wants a full outer shell.
Chlorine has seven electrons, and one empty place.
There are two ways to fill it.
Ionic on the left. Covalent on the right.
On the left, sodium meets chlorine.
Sodium has just one outer electron to lose.
So it hands it over. It is chlorine's electron now.
Chlorine turns negative. Sodium turns positive.
Opposite charges pull. That pull is the ionic bond.
On the right, chlorine meets chlorine.
Both have seven. Neither can spare one, so they
each push one electron into the space between.
That pair counts for both. Both reach eight.
Notice: no electron left. No charges. Sharing is the covalent bond.
Charges do not stop at two. Every ion pulls the
ones around it, into a giant lattice that melts high.
A shared pair is finished. You get separate molecules that melt low.
Side by side, then. Ionic is a metal
and a non-metal. Covalent is two non-metals.
Ionic gives electrons away. Covalent shares them.
Ionic makes charged ions. Covalent makes neutral molecules.
Ionic melts high. Covalent melts low.
Remember one row. Given away, or shared.
