# Ionic or covalent, told apart

1. Both bonds are atoms chasing a full outer shell. The only question is whether an electron gets handed over or held between them.
2. Sodium carries one spare electron in its outer shell. Chlorine is one electron short of a full one. Between them there is an obvious deal.
3. Chlorine pulls far harder than sodium, so it simply takes the electron. Sodium is left positive, chlorine is left negative.
4. Opposite charges attract, and that pull is the ionic bond. Millions of these ions stack into a lattice, which is why salt is a hard crystal.
5. Now take two hydrogen atoms. They pull with equal strength, so neither can take anything. They settle for holding a pair of electrons between them.
6. That shared pair is a covalent bond, and it makes a molecule, a small unit with a fixed number of atoms in it.
7. Side by side, the difference is the size of the gap in pulling power, and everything else follows from it.
8. So in the exam, check the elements first. A metal with a non-metal means transfer and ions. Two non-metals means sharing and molecules.
